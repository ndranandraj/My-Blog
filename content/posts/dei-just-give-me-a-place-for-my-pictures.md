A few years back, Instagram was a simple app. You took a picture, added a filter, and shared it. Photographers, artists and content creators loved it. It was a place to show your work and see what others were making.

Today it feels like something else. It has become a tool for propagandists.

I am an avid Instagram user. I take a lot of pictures. Birds in my backyard. A butterfly that sat still just long enough. Mountains on a road trip. The moon on a clear night. The Milky Way I spent hours capturing under a dark sky. That is what I post, and that is what I want to see.

A few weeks ago, a friend told me something that stuck with me. He said he gets bored of my nature pictures. He would rather see political content or reels. That is what keeps him hooked. Not my bird. Not my butterfly. Not my mountain. Not my Milky Way.

I laughed it off at first. Then I started thinking about it. He is not wrong about what hooks him. And that is exactly the problem.

## How the algorithm actually works

Let's keep this simple.

Every social media app has one goal. Keep you on the app as long as possible. The longer you stay, the more ads you see. The more ads you see, the more money they make.

To do that, the app watches everything you do. What you like. What you comment on. What you share. What you save. How long you stay on a post before scrolling. Even whether you paused on a video for two extra seconds.

All of this goes into a system that predicts one thing. What will keep you scrolling next?

It does not care if the content is true. It does not care if it is good for you. It only cares if you will stop and look.

## One accidental like and that's your feed forever

Here is where it gets annoying. And it's not just Instagram. Let me tell you what happened to me on X.

One day, someone I follow on X posted a picture of parotta with salna. It looked so good. Flaky, layered parotta sitting next to a bowl of hot salna. I couldn't help it. I liked it.

That was a mistake.

For the next few hours, all I saw was food. Street shop biryani. Roadside kothu parotta. Fancy plated dishes from star hotels. Dosas, burgers, desserts, everything. One like, and the algorithm decided I was a foodie now.

Another day, someone on X shared a post about a new AI model. It looked interesting, so I bookmarked it to read later. Just a bookmark. Not even a like.

Over the next few days, I got more AI content in my feed than I had seen in my entire life. New tools. New models. "This AI will replace your job" threads. Tutorials, predictions, hot takes. It never stopped.

This is called a feedback loop. You react to something. The app shows you more of it. You pause on one of those, maybe just out of curiosity. Now the app is sure. It shows you even more. The loop keeps getting tighter.

With food and AI, it is harmless. Maybe a little annoying. But the same thing happens with politics, religion and social issues. One click on a heated post, and your feed slowly fills up with the same side of the same argument.

That is why you see the same kind of content again and again. Same topics. Same opinions. Sometimes the same video posted by ten different accounts.

## The trap you don't notice

The scary part is that you don't feel trapped. It feels normal. It feels like the world agrees with you.

But the algorithm has quietly removed everything else. The other side of the argument never shows up. Not because it doesn't exist. Because the app decided you wouldn't click on it.

Over time, you start believing that everyone thinks the way you do. And when you meet someone who doesn't, they seem strange. Or stupid. Or dangerous.

This is the echo chamber. You hear your own voice bouncing back at you, louder each time.

I want the opposite. I want to see views I disagree with. Even better, ones that prove me wrong. That is how you learn. Nobody ever grew by hearing only what they already believed.

## Us vs the world

Let me say it plainly. This algorithm is making us dumb.

Not because we are not smart. Because we stopped hearing anything new. When you only get fed what you already agree with, your brain stops working hard. You stop questioning. You stop thinking, "Wait, what if the other person has a point?"

The algorithm doesn't show you alternate ways of looking at things. It doesn't help you understand why someone believes something different. It only shows you why they are wrong.

Growing up, we learned to live with people who thought differently. Different religions, different politics, different food, different languages. We argued, we laughed, and we still sat together for tea. That is what coexisting looks like.

The feed doesn't allow that. It keeps pushing one message. Us vs them. My side is right. Your side is the enemy. Every day, one more post telling you the other side is out to get you.

Maybe no engineer sat down and planned it this way. But the result is the same. Fear and anger keep people scrolling. And an "us vs the world" mindset is the easiest way to keep people afraid and angry.

## Bad news travels faster

Here is another thing I noticed. Negative content spreads much faster than positive content.

Think about it. A calm post about a sunrise gets a few likes. A post that makes you angry gets hundreds of comments, shares and quote posts. People fight in the replies. Others jump in to take sides. All that activity tells the algorithm, "People love this. Show it to more people."

So the angry post wins. Every single time.

Positive content doesn't stand a chance. A kind story, a beautiful picture, someone sharing something they learned. These things quietly get buried. Not because people don't like them. Because they don't make people react as strongly.

The end result? Open the app and most of what you see is bad. Fights. Scandals. Crimes. Someone insulting someone. Some new reason to be scared or furious.

And it slowly does something to your mind. You close the app feeling worse than when you opened it. Sad. Angry. Anxious. Pissed off for no clear reason. You didn't have a bad day. You just scrolled through one.

Do that every day, for hours, and it starts to feel like the whole world is falling apart. It isn't. You are just being shown the worst of it, again and again, because the worst of it keeps you hooked.

## What this means for society

Now multiply this by a billion people.

Each person is sitting in their own little bubble. Each bubble is sure it is right. Nobody is talking across bubbles. They are only shouting.

The algorithm rewards the loudest, angriest content. People learn this. Creators learn this. Politicians learn this. Everyone starts playing to the algorithm.

The result is a society that is more divided, more angry and less willing to listen.

## How narratives get pushed

Here is the part that should worry all of us.

If you know how the algorithm works, you can use it. And many people do.

Political parties, interest groups and even foreign actors run organized campaigns. They create hundreds of accounts. They post the same message in slightly different ways. They like and share each other's posts to push them up. The algorithm sees all this activity and thinks, "This must be popular." So it shows it to more people.

Suddenly a narrative looks like it came from the public. In reality, it was planned.

This is how opinions get shaped before an election. How a policy gets support or gets killed. How one version of an event becomes "the truth" in a few hours. The people pushing the story don't need to control the news anymore. They just need to understand the feed.

And the platform? It doesn't mind. Engagement is engagement.

## So what would a better platform look like?

I have been thinking about this a lot. Here is what I believe it needs.

**A chronological feed.** Show me posts from the people I follow, in the order they were posted. That's it. Instagram actually worked this way in the beginning. It changed in 2016. I miss it.

**You pick what you follow, not the app.** No "suggested for you." No reels from strangers taking over my screen. If I want to discover new people, give me a search bar and let me explore on my own terms.

**No hidden ranking.** If there is any sorting at all, tell me exactly how it works. Let me turn it off.

**A different business model.** As long as the platform earns money from ads, it will always want you hooked. A small subscription, or a community funded model, removes that pressure. When you are the customer and not the product, the incentives change.

**Separate spaces for separate things.** A place for pictures should be for pictures. A place for news and debate can be somewhere else. Mixing everything together is how my Milky Way shot ends up sandwiched between two political rants.

**Room for the other side.** A good platform could even do the opposite of today's apps. Once in a while, show me a well made argument from a view I don't hold. Not to change my mind. Just so I know it exists.

Some platforms are already trying pieces of this. Open networks like Mastodon and Bluesky let users choose or build their own feeds. Pixelfed tries to be a simple photo sharing space without the heavy algorithm. None of them are perfect yet. But they prove the idea is possible.

## Letting things flow naturally

The internet used to feel like a big open street. You walked around, you bumped into things, you found stuff you never knew you needed. Today it feels like a store where the aisles keep rearranging to push you toward the same shelf.

I don't want a feed that knows me too well. I want one that surprises me.

So to my friend who finds my nature pictures boring, fair enough. But I will keep posting them. The bird, the butterfly, the mountain and the Milky Way. Because they were real moments, not something an algorithm decided I should see.

And if someone out there is building that simple, honest platform for pictures, please let me know. I will be your first user.
