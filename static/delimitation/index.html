How India divides Lok Sabha seats

India's seat allocation comes from a rule the Delimitation Commission wrote down in 1963: divide by the average, round to the nearest whole number, and if the total falls short, give the spare seat to the largest fraction below one half. Each line is one unit. Under a largest-remainder rule such as India's, a line can go down as the House grows. That is the Alabama paradox, and it is why the United States abandoned this method in 1900. Divisor methods never do it.

What this is. A calculator, not a prediction. India has not moved seats between states since 1976, so every figure other than the "Actual" column is a counterfactual: what the chosen rule produces on the chosen census at the chosen House size.

Units. Each census uses the units of its own time. The 2011 map has undivided Andhra Pradesh and pre-2019 Jammu & Kashmir, so it is not today's map. The 1976 Order's own total is 542, not 543; the extra seat is Goa, which became a separate state and union territory in 1987.

The six-million floor is the proviso to article 81(2), inserted in 1973 and tied by article 81(3) to the 1971 census. Applying it to any other census is a modelling choice, and this tool flags it when you do.
